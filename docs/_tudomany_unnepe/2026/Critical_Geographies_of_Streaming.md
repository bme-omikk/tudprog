The Budapest University of Technology and Economics Department of Sociology and Communication presents:

Critical Geographies of Streaming: Music, Film and Television in the Global East
Petr Szczepanik (Charles University, Prague)

Streaming platforms integrate Central and Eastern Europe (CEE) into global cultural markets on unequal terms. This talk develops a critical geography of streaming across music, film and television through Martin Müller’s concept of the Global East. This relational perspective brings intra-European dependencies into decolonization debates and questions a North–South framing that often leaves postsocialist experience underexamined.

Drawing on research with Miloš Hroch, the first part introduces concepts that connect spatial, temporal, and subjective dimensions of platformization across scales. Subordinated proximity names a CEE modality of the Global East: formal access to Western cultural and platform circuits alongside unequal power over visibility and recognition. Spatial gatekeeping describes how platforms and intermediaries reproduce this position by organizing visibility geographically. Industry workshops and interviews with Czech musicians illustrate how these inequalities become professional expectations and lived pressures. They inform the concept of algorithmic catching up: the postsocialist demand to become Western reformulated as platform readiness. Spotify playlisting can promise a route beyond postsocialist peripherality, while weak managerial and export infrastructures limit artists’ capacity to turn visibility into sustainable international careers. Peripheral platform subjectivation captures their negotiation of these demands. Optimization can coexist with fatigue and selective withdrawal; local identity becomes an ambivalent resource for international recognition.
The second part examines how subscription video platforms reorganize CEE markets and how regional actors respond. The industry’s “land of bad TV” trope casts CEE as culturally deficient, legitimizing low-risk extraction through cheap licensing and service production alongside selective upgrading. Drawing on postcolonial accounts of CEE as “conditionally European”, I examine how such imaginaries authorize extractive economic practices. The trope locates “quality TV” in Western Europe and the US; CEE is then presented as an exploitation market requiring professional instruction, with quality measured against external benchmarks. Western platforms’ claims to modernize cultural infrastructure can obscure dependency, value extraction and data capture, while displacing authority over cultural value. Netflix’s Polish commissioning hub illustrates selective upgrading. I read Prague’s service-production economy through the concept of a strain-absorbing periphery, where production growth can coexist with risk and labour stress displaced from core markets. Oneplay, the dominant Czech streaming service, integrates streaming with television and telecommunications, showing how regional actors pursue their own consolidation strategies in response to global platforms.

The talk concludes with a programme of conjunctural comparison that uses this regional positionality to theorize the global platform economy relationally. It connects ownership and rights to interface visibility and recommendation. Across this digital stack, comparison asks how shared platform pressures take distinct forms through local histories and unequal relations between places.

The lecture will be moderated by Emília Barna (Budapest University of Technology and Economics).

About the presenter:

Petr Szczepanik is an Associate Professor at Charles University (Prague, Czech Republic), specializing in East-Central European screen industries, production cultures, and critical geography of streaming economies. He co-edited Behind the Screen (Palgrave, 2013) and Digital Peripheries (Springer, 2020), and authored Screen Industries in East-Central Europe (Bloomsbury, 2021). His current research examines how platformization reproduces and reshapes geographic inequalities in global media industries.
